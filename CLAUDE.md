## Stack
- Plain static HTML/CSS/JS in `public/`. No framework, no build step. Keep it that way unless asked.
- Hosted on Cloudflare Workers with static assets (`wrangler.jsonc`). `worker/index.js` only handles `/api/*`; everything else is a static file.
- Domain: parahouse.co, registered at Porkbun (renews yearly, auto-renew on), nameservers on Cloudflare. `wrangler.jsonc` `routes` attach parahouse.co and www.parahouse.co to the Worker as custom domains on every deploy.
- Contact form posts to `/api/contact`, which emails the brief with Cloudflare Email Service (`send_email` binding `EMAIL` in `wrangler.jsonc`, locked to the verified Email Routing destination parahouse0@gmail.com; free, no API key, no Resend). Vars: `CONTACT_TO` (parahouse0@gmail.com), `CONTACT_FROM` (website@parahouse.co). Reply-to is the visitor, so replying in Gmail answers them. Incoming mail to hello@parahouse.co is forwarded to Gmail by Cloudflare Email Routing. Nicholas sends and replies as hello@parahouse.co from Gmail ("Send mail as", default address) through Resend SMTP; Resend is verified for parahouse.co (Tokyo) on the `send.` subdomain and is not used by the site. DNS also has SPF (Cloudflare routing), Resend DKIM and DMARC `v=DMARC1; p=none;` (can move to quarantine once mail has run cleanly for a few weeks).

- Live visitor count (2026-10-23): every page sends an anonymous ping every 30s on the live site only (`site.js`, sendBeacon to `/api/ping` with a random per-tab id from sessionStorage and the path; no cookies, no IP kept). A Durable Object `Presence` (`worker/index.js`, binding `PRESENCE`) keeps tabs heard from in the last 65s in memory. `/api/live?k=<private key>` shows the count and pages, refreshing every 5s; only the key's SHA-256 is in `wrangler.jsonc` (`LIVE_KEY_HASH`), the key itself is with Nicholas, never put it in this repo. Private on purpose: no visitor counts on the public site.

## Copy
- PROJECT PAGES, the standing rule (Nicholas, for every project): write as if for the creative founder of an agency who sees 200 portfolios a day, and answer "why would they choose us?". The punchline is the idea in a few words. The story proves execution with concrete, named elements, how it went from render to build (one set of files), and the value or a real result. Specific over abstract (never "engaging spatial experiences", "journey of discovery"), short over complete, no invented outcomes. Nicholas supplies the context; edit his copy through this lens and say what changed.
- parahouse is premium, in a quiet-luxury way: the site never talks about money. No prices, rates, fees, discounts, credits, "cheaper", salaries or "no obligation", and no explaining why there are no prices. Commercial terms (pitch fees, the partial credit if a pitch is awarded, rates) are discussed privately after a brief. Argue value: judgement, real-dimension design, one team from pitch to build, a few projects at a time.
- No email address or phone number anywhere on the site, including error messages: every enquiry goes through the brief form.
- No decorative numbers or counts anywhere (no "(16)" beside headings, no "4+", no 001/01 indexes). Nicholas has designed for more brands than are listed, so a count undersells him.
- Written to agencies, and to three of them equally (never pitch-only): agencies pitching for a project, agencies that have won one and need extra hands to deliver it (on the site, "new projects", not "won projects"), and agencies with no creative team of their own who want an experienced one without hiring. Name all three wherever the site says who it is for (Services lede, Contact lede, About Capacity, the FAQ). The reader is an account director or creative lead. Say "your client", "your team", "your fabricator"; never address the brand directly. Cover creative work as a whole, 2D and 3D (concept, 3D and rendering, 2D graphics, drawings and artwork), not just 3D. Be sharp about the agency's return: pitches that win, capacity without headcount, the client relationship and margin stay theirs, fewer costly surprises on site. Key promises: we work under your name, we don't contact your client unless asked, design your fabricator and printer can produce.
- Promises Nicholas has given: first renders usually within 48 hours of the brief, with the timeline confirmed in the reply (stated in the Services FAQ; softened 2026-10-16) and a reply to every brief within one working day (Contact lede). Don't promise other turnaround times, revision rounds or on-site work unless he gives them.
- CONCISE and plain on the main pages (Home, About, Services, Contact) (Nicholas, 2026-10-16; project page descriptions keep their fuller three-paragraph story, he reverted the shortened ones): say the point in as few words as possible, no roundabout openers ("We're a … and agencies are who we work for"), no fancy or poetic phrasing, no warm-up clauses. Short sentences. Genuine is fine: a limit can be stated honestly, briefly, then the point ("Sometimes one freelancer is enough. We're for when the idea still needs solving."). Never invent the limit or the point. Write in sentence case; CSS handles the uppercase for headings and labels.
- Calls to action: "I would like to explore my options" (footer and the monthly retainers) and "Send brief" (form and project bundles). Contact person: Nicholas (not named on the site).
