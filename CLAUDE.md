# parahouse site: notes for Claude Code

Studio website for parahouse (always lowercase), a Singapore design studio that works for agencies (not brands) on roadshows, exhibitions and brand events: concepts, 2D and 3D design, renders, technical drawings and print-ready artwork, delivered under the agency's name. parahouse is digital only; fabrication is the agency's fabricator. Positioning: "Your silent backend. We don't compete, we enable." Tagline (deck): "We build fast. You look good." Not used on the site, since parahouse doesn't build. Source of truth for copy is the 2026 capabilities deck.

## Stack
- Plain static HTML/CSS/JS in `public/`. No framework, no build step. Keep it that way unless asked.
- Hosted on Cloudflare Workers with static assets (`wrangler.jsonc`). `worker/index.js` only handles `/api/*`; everything else is a static file.
- Domain: parahouse.co, registered at Porkbun (renews yearly, auto-renew on), nameservers on Cloudflare. `wrangler.jsonc` `routes` attach parahouse.co and www.parahouse.co to the Worker as custom domains on every deploy.
- Contact form posts to `/api/contact`, which emails the brief with Cloudflare Email Service (`send_email` binding `EMAIL` in `wrangler.jsonc`, locked to the verified Email Routing destination parahouse0@gmail.com; free, no API key, no Resend). Vars: `CONTACT_TO` (parahouse0@gmail.com), `CONTACT_FROM` (website@parahouse.co). Reply-to is the visitor, so replying in Gmail answers them. Incoming mail to hello@parahouse.co is forwarded to Gmail by Cloudflare Email Routing. Nicholas sends and replies as hello@parahouse.co from Gmail ("Send mail as", default address) through Resend SMTP; Resend is verified for parahouse.co (Tokyo) on the `send.` subdomain and is not used by the site. DNS also has SPF (Cloudflare routing), Resend DKIM and DMARC `v=DMARC1; p=none;` (can move to quarantine once mail has run cleanly for a few weeks).

## Design rules (references: Zeroframe first, then Foucault)
- White site. Colour relationship after Nicholas's reference, the Kinetic SMM studio site on Behance (colour only, not its layout): pure white ground (`--bg` #fff, no gradient or tint anywhere, project pages included; `body::before` is off), deep black (`--fg` #0d0d0d) for text and SOLID cards (Home's project cards are black with white type), neutral grey for secondary text (`--muted` #6e6e6e), soft surfaces (`--surface` #efefef: Services cards, fields, About P pieces), client logos (`--logo`) and the giant footer wordmark (#dcdcdc), and ONE accent used sparingly: Kinetic's orange is replaced by a deep lipstick red (`--brand` #c8102e, white text on it; also `--brand-text`) for the P logo, dots, the round and pill buttons, offer + marks, the current page, small labels and the About glow. Headlines can be two-tone like the reference: black, then grey (`.tone`, `--tone` #8c8c8c, large text only), as in Home's intro line. The menu panel and the page-change curtain are deep black. Tokens are at the top of `public/assets/css/site.css`; use them. Project pages keep their own colours exactly (Nicholas asked for this), set on each page's `<html>`: `--brand` (the project's colour, unchanged: logo, fills, glows, round button, with `--brand-ink` #0b0b0b on it), `--brand-text` (the same hue darkened until it passes 4.5:1 as text on white), `--curtain` (unchanged); `--bg-top` is unused. `window.PH_CURTAINS` maps project URLs to their curtain colour; every other page's curtain is #111111. `theme-color` is #ffffff on every page.
- One fixed look: no time-of-day lighting and no light/dark toggle (removed at Nicholas's request; night was too dim).
- Type, after the same reference: Inter (variable 300–700, self-hosted `inter-var.woff2`, OFL, preloaded in every `<head>`) for everything: headings, body, labels. Headings in sentence case, weight 500, tight tracking (-0.035em), no caps or extended widths (one override block at the end of site.css lists every heading selector). Small labels stay small uppercase with letter-spacing. The footer wordmark is lowercase, 20vw, weight 600, tracking -0.06em, neutral grey, so it fits from 390px to wide screens.
- Brand red `--brand` (#c8102e, a deep lipstick red in place of the reference's orange; white text on it) is the only accent, used sparingly: the P logo, buttons, dots, the current page, labels, glows. On project pages it becomes the project's colour (see above).
- Logo: the P mark in the accent colour (red; a project's colour on its page) (inline SVG, a traced stand-in until the original file is supplied), top-left, no text. On hover its two parts (`.p-head`, `.p-stem`) spring apart and back.
- Navigation: at the top of every page, Work / Services / About / Contact stack downwards top-right in large uppercase Geist (`.topnav`, current page in red with a dot to its left). After 120px of scrolling `site.js` sets `html.is-scrolled`; the links fade out and the glassy Menu pill (`.menu-toggle`) scales in, opening a compact panel (`#menu`). On phones only the Menu pill shows.
- Renders are the face of every project (no block massing). Each project's cover is its `cover` image (default `<slug>/render.webp`); home cards and the footer strip use it, with a slight zoom on hover. Event photos are supporting proof on project pages, labelled "On site".
- Home Selected work copies the portfolio cards on fabric.codebydennis.com (Nicholas asked for a close copy, small glitches included): `.pcards` holds a `.pcard-cell` for every project (each with `data-client`), and three are shown at a time in three columns split by hairlines (stacked on phones). An inline script right after the list picks three at random on every visit, each from a different client, before the first paint (Nicholas asked for this instead of a fixed top three). Without JavaScript the three without `hidden` show: Cetaphil, ORA 5 and Don Julio. A new project gets a cell here too, `hidden`. The pill shows the project's category. Each card: a light pill with the project label, the client logo (white mask from the brand files; the client name in display type when there's no file), client name, title in grey, and a bottom bar "View project" with a round arrow. On hover the project's cover image fades in behind the text (darkened so the text stays readable), the card lifts 7px up and right and leaves an outline where it was, the pill text slides up and is replaced by its copy, the arrow slides out and back in, name and title scramble (`data-scramble`, characters `XYZxyzabc0$€£`, 0.6s, on enter and leave), and a dark pill follows the cursor with the project name, scrambled in (`data-cursor-text`; it sits left of the cursor on right-hand cards). The section heading scrambles in when scrolled into view (`data-scroll-scramble`). On touch screens (`hover: none`) there is no hover: each card shows its image from the start, with no lift, scramble or cursor pill (one CSS rule, plus the scramble listeners only attach on hover devices). The old flipping shelf was removed as too distracting. The cards float into place as they scroll in (after lusion.co's featured work): `site.js` moves each `.pcard` (and each Work page `.work-card`) from 110px low at 88% size to its spot as it rises through the screen, eased so the card trails the scroll slightly and catches up; columns arrive one after another. JavaScript, not CSS scroll timelines, because Safari before 26 has no scroll timelines. Works at every width; off with reduced motion. Their pill shows an index number (N.001); ours shows the project type instead, per the no-numbers rule.
- Brand carousel: client logo files supplied by Nicholas, in `public/assets/img/brands/<slug>.svg|png|webp` (names in that folder's README), with no boxes and generous space between logos, drifting slowly left (pure CSS, two copies for a seamless loop, keeps moving on hover; with reduced motion a still wrapping grid). They render as masks in grey (`--logo`) and turn near-black on hover. A brand without a file shows its name as a small mono placeholder. Only use logo files Nicholas provides; never draw or trace one.
- Images sit in rounded tiles (`--radius`) with a tight 8px gap.
- A small near-black dot trails the cursor with a slight delay on every page (`.cursor-dot`, faint shadow for light backgrounds). It settles just above and to the right of the pointer (+16px, -14px), never on top of it; the cursor pill sits below. Pointer devices only, no delay with reduced motion.
- Motion only answers the visitor or marks one moment: hero crossfade, menu opening, tile hover, sections fading up on scroll, and the curtain. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (the curtain and intro are skipped entirely).
- Curtain (after Dennis Snellenberg): first visit per browser session shows the intro: a deep red screen (`--curtain`) cycles "• Hello" through greetings, then lifts with a curved bottom edge. Internal links raise the curtain with a curved top edge and "• <page name>", then navigate; the next page opens covered and lifts. The `<head>` script sets `is-intro` / `is-entering` and `data-curtain` before paint; CSS does the lifting, `site.js` only cycles words and handles leaving (navigates when the rising curtain's animation ends). The leaving and arriving labels sit at exactly the same spot so the hand-off is invisible. Pages are prefetched on hover (speculation rules in `<head>`), and a cross-document view transition holds the frame between pages so there's no flash. Link labels come from `data-label` (project links use the client name) or the page name.

## Copy
- parahouse is premium, in a quiet-luxury way: the site never talks about money. No prices, rates, fees, discounts, credits, "cheaper", salaries or "no obligation", and no explaining why there are no prices. Commercial terms (pitch fees, the partial credit if a pitch is awarded, rates) are discussed privately after a brief. Argue value: judgement, real-dimension design, one team from pitch to build, a few projects at a time.
- No email address or phone number anywhere on the site, including error messages: every enquiry goes through the brief form.
- No decorative numbers or counts anywhere (no "(16)" beside headings, no "4+", no 001/01 indexes). Nicholas has designed for more brands than are listed, so a count undersells him.
- Written to agencies, and to three of them equally (never pitch-only): agencies pitching for a project, agencies that have won one and need extra hands to deliver it, and agencies with no creative team of their own who want an experienced one without hiring. Name all three wherever the site says who it is for (Services lede, Contact lede, About Capacity, the FAQ). The reader is an account director or creative lead. Say "your client", "your team", "your fabricator"; never address the brand directly. Cover creative work as a whole, 2D and 3D (concept, 3D and rendering, 2D graphics, drawings and artwork), not just 3D. Be sharp about the agency's return: pitches that win, capacity without headcount, the client relationship and margin stay theirs, fewer costly surprises on site. Key promises: we work under your name, we don't contact your client unless asked, design your fabricator and printer can produce.
- Promises Nicholas has given: first renders within 48 hours of the brief (stated on the Pitch Kit) and a reply to every brief within one working day (Contact lede). Don't promise other turnaround times, revision rounds or on-site work unless he gives them.
- Plain, specific, no hype words. Write in sentence case; CSS handles the uppercase for headings and labels.
- Calls to action: "Let’s talk" (footer) and "Send brief" (form). Contact person: Nicholas (not named on the site).

## Every page shares
The same `<head>` (including the inline theme script), the floating controls, the `#menu` panel and the footer (not on Home). If you change one, change all of them (`public/**/*.html`, including `404.html`).

Project pages with a real, stated outcome show it under the meta grid as `.result` (Result: one line, in the project colour). Only use outcomes Nicholas has stated; never invent one. Projects without one leave it out.

## Adding a project
1. Copy a folder in `public/portfolio/` and rename it with a lowercase-hyphen slug.
2. Update: `<title>`, meta description, `og:` tags, `<h1>`, the four meta items (Client, Event, Type, Scope), summary, body, image paths and alt text, and pick the project's `hue` from its main colour (judge by eye from the renders; an automatic average gets fooled by carpets and studio walls).
3. Images go in their own folder, `public/assets/img/projects/<slug>/`: `cover.webp` (16:10 crop, for cards, the home hero and the Work grid), `second.webp` (footer strip) and `img-01.webp`… for the project page at the photos' original proportions (no cropping; Nicholas asked for this). Instagram collage posts are split into their panels, each kept at its own shape. On the page (`.media.mosaic`): `full` spans the row, `duo` pairs two images of the same shape, `solo` centres a single portrait. Up to 2000 px, under 400 KB. Renders first ("The render"), then photos ("The build"), or one "The event" section for photo-only projects. Every image Nicholas shares goes on the page; none are left out.
4. Add a `.work-card` to `public/portfolio/index.html` with its category in `data-type` (`roadshow` | `brand-events` | `product` | `exhibition` | `multimedia`) and the same category under Type on its page. Also add its `.pcard-cell` (with `data-client` and `hidden`) to Home's list so it can be picked at random.
5. Optionally add it to the home showcase: one `<img>` in `[data-showcase-media]` and one `<li>` in `[data-showcase-list]`, in the same position. The link's `data-meta` fills the top-right label.
6. Fix the `.next-pill` links so every project points to the next and the last loops to the first.

## Processing a submission
New projects arrive in `submissions/<slug>/` (outside `public/`, so never published): `project.md` (filled from `_template/project.md`), `renders/`, `photos/`.
1. Read `project.md`. If "OK to show publicly" is No, stop and ask Nicholas.
2. Pick the strongest render as the cover; use every render and photo Nicholas shares on the project page, renders first. Never leave an image out, even if it looks repetitive (Nicholas asked for this); order and pairing are the place to handle repetition. Show Nicholas a contact sheet of the picks before publishing if the choice isn't obvious.
3. Resize to 2000 px on the long side (no cropping), WebP, under 400 KB, into `public/assets/img/projects/<slug>/`. Use only the images Nicholas wants shown (for ORA 5, launch day only; the pre-launch set is not used).
4. Build the project page and tile as in "Adding a project", using his wording, tightened. Add its `.pcard-cell` to Home's random pool.
5. Delete the `submissions/<slug>/` folder in the same commit.
